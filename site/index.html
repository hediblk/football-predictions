The model

How the forecasts are made

Rate every team from past results and expected goals, price each remaining match, then play the rest of the season out 10,000 times. Every percentage on this site is a share of those simulated seasons.

10K
Three steps

  1. 01Rate the teams

    A Dixon–Coles goal model fits each team's attack and defence from its goals, counting recent matches more: a game a year old weighs about a third as much as last weekend's. A quarter of that strength comes from real goals and three quarters from the same model fitted to expected goals (xG), which is steadier over a few games. An Elo rating tracks results alone.

  2. 02Price every match

    Both models give home, draw and away chances for each remaining fixture. They are blended in log space, 84% Dixon–Coles and 16% Elo, and the goal model supplies the likely scorelines within each result.

  3. 03Play out the season

    Each simulated season draws a result and a score for every fixture left, adds up points and goals, and ranks the table with the competition's own tie-breakers (La Liga, for one, settles level points on head-to-head). Ten thousand seasons give the chance of every finishing place.

Reading the numbers
Ranges
Point ranges cover the middle 80% of simulated seasons (10th to 90th percentile). They show match-to-match luck, not doubt about the ratings themselves.
Zones
Title, Europe and Down are finishing places. Real European spots also depend on cup winners, and deciding play-offs are not simulated.
Track record
Every played match is forecast at 00:00 UTC on its match day from earlier results only, so the record on Matches never peeks at the result. A call is right when the model's likeliest outcome happens.
Champions League
One fit
Domestic and European results from every entrant's league feed a single goal model. Country offsets put leagues on a common scale, and European games get their own scoring and home baselines. Bookmaker prices for upcoming Champions League games set each league's current level, since two seasons of European results can lag behind.
League phase
All 144 fixtures are played out and ranked with UEFA's tie-breakers, including opponents' combined points.
Knockouts
Draws follow the fixed bracket and seeding. Extra time runs at a third of the second leg's scoring rate and shoot-outs are a coin toss. The projected bracket is one illustrative path; title chances average every simulated draw.
Club ratings
Rating
The share of the points a club would take off an average European top-flight side on a neutral pitch, from the model behind the Champions League forecasts: domestic and European results, goals and expected goals, recent games counting more.
Attack, defence
Attack rates a club as if its defence were exactly as good as its attack, and defence the reverse, so all three numbers share one scale. Scores and Concedes are goals per game against that average side.
Across leagues
Leagues are compared through matches between clubs from different countries in the European cups; a league with few such matches is assumed to be a typical European league until results say otherwise. Bookmaker prices for upcoming Champions League games then set each league's current level. The average side is the mean of every club with domestic results in the past year. Baselines and coverage

Prior frequency is fixed from the five seasons before this one, with seasons listed above. It is unavailable until all five are loaded. Elo compares only fixtures with a saved component from the same forecast run.

Published forecasts were saved before kickoff. Reconstructed forecasts use results before match day and fill games without a receipt; the combined record counts each fixture once. Calibrated seasons are not independent holdouts. Champions League strengths already use archived market prices. Market comparison awaits enough archived quotes.

Limits
Fixed ratings
Chances assume today's ratings are right. Transfers, injuries and managerial changes only show up once they change results.
Calibration
Weights were tuned on single matches, not on final standings.
Data gaps
Cancelled fixtures are played as if rescheduled. Expected goals cover only the top five leagues, so Champions League sides from elsewhere are rated on goals.
