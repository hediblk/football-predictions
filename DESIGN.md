# Floodlight — design DNA

Chosen from four dealt directions (design-for-ai dealer, project `final-table-football-forecasts`, 2026-10-11, hand 3). Dark only, by request.

## Scoreboard

GROUNDING   1980s stadium electronic scoreboards' dot-matrix bulb numerals + Bloomberg Terminal's dense, ruled tables.
The page reads like the board at the ground: one lit number you see from the stands, then rows of small print you lean in for.

TYPE        Big Shoulders Display (condensed civic signage, 600–900) for names, labels and the hero numeral, over JetBrains Mono (400–700) for every number and running text. Hierarchy by weight, caps and tracking, not size steps; one huge jump for the hero.
COLOR       seed amber (h68) · warm near-black ground · mono harmony · one lit accent (amber) plus a red bulb reserved for relegation/elimination.
COMPOSITION Poster Bleed (Terminal/Mono-Core) — the favourite's percentage is set at poster scale and bleeds off the frame's right edge; everything else is caption-rank and ruled.
MOTION      State-only and short: the hero numeral "powers on" in four steps (≤150 ms, stepped) when the competition changes; changing match day slides the day strip and the fixtures across (260 ms ease-out), and the strip can be dragged. Nothing else moves. Off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
REGISTER    Product structure (tables, nav) · expressive at: the hero numeral only.
DNA         Terminal/Mono-Core base + type voice borrowed from Swiss/International (a condensed grotesk instead of mono display) · dominant: color (the single lit amber).
SIGNATURE   Section headings are legends cut into their frame's top border, like the labels on a stadium board; probabilities are drawn as bulbs whose brightness is the chance.

## Tokens

```css
--bg:      #131312;  /* page */
--panel:   #1a1919;  /* frames, sticky cells */
--bulb-off:#221f1b;  /* unlit bulb */
--rule:    #3f3b36;  /* frame borders, cut lines */
--rule-2:  #262421;  /* row rules */
--ink:     #ebe7e3;  /* primary text, 14.3:1 on panel */
--ink-2:   #bcb6b0;  /* secondary text, 8.7:1 */
--dim:     #8a837c;  /* labels, 4.9:1 */
--amber:   #eaa85e;  /* the lit accent */
--amber-t: #d7af86;  /* amber text on dark, 8.7:1 */
--red:     #e1524f;  /* relegation / elimination bulb; a missed call */
--green:   #5ad664;  /* a called result, or a rank gained: lamp / arrow */
--green-t: #88cb8a;  /* "Called" text */
--draw:    #8a8f98;  /* a drawn game: form boxes and dots */
--yellow:  #f2c14e;  /* a yellow card icon only */
```

`--green` came from the same generator (`--seed 145`, accent steps 9 and 11); it marks a called result and, with `--green-t`, a rank gained. Calls always pair a lamp with a word (Called, Missed, Exact), never colour alone.

## Components added with Matches and Clubs

- **Club crests:** full-colour original artwork beside club names; 18 px inline, 24 px in rows and brackets, 36 px on cards, 80 px in heroes (64 px on phones). Preserve proportions and keep the club name visible. Use published light or outlined variants for dark artwork; keep crest containers transparent. Charts keep their dots, with crests in hover details; native dropdown options remain text with a crest beside the selected club.

- **Logo:** the favicon's 3×3 bulb grid (five lit, four off) beside the Floodlight wordmark, in the header and the footer.
- **Section tabs** (Tables, Matches, Clubs, Backtest, Model) sit beside the logo; the competition row appears only under Tables. Methodology lives only on Model; other views link to it.
- **Footer:** brand, Project links (Model, GitHub, roadmap) and Data links to every provider, above the model-run time.
- **Outcome bulbs:** a match's home/draw/away chances are three small bulbs whose brightness is the chance; the brightest is the model's pick, and a ruled box marks what actually happened.
- **Forecast origin:** the dim line in a finished match's "The call" block names a saved pre-kickoff forecast as "Published before kickoff", with the model run's UTC time and date. Otherwise it says "Reconstructed", using results before 00:00 UTC on match day.
- **Day strip:** nine days with big numerals and a game count; the chosen day is lit and underlined in amber.
- **Call lens:** a "Judge calls by" chip pair (Score / xG) under the Matches competition chips. Score is the default. xG judges each played game by the exported `miss.xg`: Called when the model's favourite out-xG'd its opponent by more than 0.5, Missed when the opponent did, Level (grey lamp) within 0.5, and "No xG" where there is none. League xG is Understat's and Champions League xG is ESPN's; every xG label names its source. The day summary and the scorecard's Called column follow the lens and leave level and uncovered games out of the rate; the round card and club pages stay on the score.
- **Call strip:** on a club page, one bar per played game; its height is the chance the model gave what happened, green when that was the pick and red when not, so it agrees with the "Called N of M" line.
- **Results against the model:** above the call strip, cumulative actual points use a solid ink line and model-expected points a dashed secondary-ink line. The summary gives actual points, model expectation, signed difference and published/reconstructed forecast counts. Competition chips keep the series separate, opening on the domestic league; Champions League points cover the league phase. A game-by-game table is the chart's text equivalent.
- **Rolling xG:** the Goals frame shows last-five and last-ten xG for, against and difference per covered game. Each window states its xG coverage; the five-game difference sparkline leaves windows without xG blank and has an expandable value table.
- **Table vs model:** after current points, a signed one-decimal column shows match points above or below the model's expected points. The table note defines expected points as `3 × P(win) + P(draw)` and states that the comparison uses finished games with a forecast; Champions League values use league-phase matches.
- **Rank plates:** category ranks (#1 attack, #1 defence, 1st in league) sit on a lit amber plate; top-ten ranks are amber text; the rest stay dim.
- **Stakes:** a fixture's conditional chances for each result, with the likeliest result lit amber. They're shown only when a result swings a chance by at least 2 points and by more than three standard errors, so simulation noise never reads as stakes. On top of that, a title swing must be 8 points or more and a top-four or relegation swing 15, so only games that really matter carry a Stakes tag, a stakes table or a place in "Games that decide our season". Champions League games use the same trophy bar (8) but 30 points for the top eight and top 24, since one league-phase game moves those by 25 or more almost every time; a club in both lists its league and Champions League games under separate ruled headings.
- **Club ranking:** follows the strength hero directly, with ratings and attack/defence hairline meters; there is no separate Big clubs board. Champions League clubs from outside the five leagues show a country code where a league would go, and their pages follow the Champions League season.
- **Movement arrows:** weekly rank changes use a green ▲ with the number of places gained or a red ▼ with the places dropped, a dim dash when unchanged and dim "new" for clubs without a previous week; colour never stands alone. This extends `--green`/`--green-t` (otherwise reserved for called results) and `--red` (relegation, misses) to rank movement.
- **Rating history and comparison:** the club profile's Strength frame adds signed rating changes since the previous frozen Monday, a weekly Overall · Attack · Defence line chart with a live endpoint, and a native league-grouped comparison select. The club line is amber; a comparison uses dashed ink and is named at the line end. Each gauge adds an outlined tick with the other club's printed value; the thin solid tick still marks the European average. A caption and expandable weekly-values table describe the chart, and the shareable route is `#clubs/<slug>/vs/<other slug>`. The chart's axis spans the plotted values in steps of ten so weekly moves stay visible, and changing the comparison keeps the select where it was on screen instead of returning to the top.
- **Form:** results use the standard colours: win green, draw grey (`--draw`), loss red. The club page shows its last six as lettered boxes; the standings (a Form column) and an opened match (under both clubs' names, as they stood before kickoff) show the last five as 7 px dots with the result in a hover tip and a spoken label.
- **Form extras:** match rows carry both clubs' dots under their names, reserving the 24 px name/crest line and 7 px dot line even without history. Rows share their list's columns; Stakes has a reserved slot on desktop so its absence never shifts the teams or probabilities. On phones, probabilities and expected goals share one line, with the verdict and Stakes below. Opened panels add streaks (3+ wins or losses, 4+ unbeaten or winless) and recent meetings this season; the club hero shows dots and the streak, and the season card home and away dots. Fixture strength (`#rank`, red for the top ten, green for the bottom thirty) sits beside each upcoming opponent; the number is always shown. Standings headers sort descending on press (press again to restore); cut lines hide while sorted.
- **Race charts:** chances through the season as thin lines on the ruled grid. The leader is lit amber (red for relegation) and the others are ink with dash patterns, never extra hues. Names sit at the line ends.
- **Race movers:** inside "The race so far", between the chips and the chart, "Movers since Monday <date>" lists up to five clubs whose chance in the chosen race moved by 3 points or more since the Clubs ranking's baseline (the history point on the last date before the Monday frozen before this week), biggest first. Each `.moves` row has crest and name, dim "before → now", a diverging hairline bar from a centre tick and the ▲▼ signed points. Colour says good or bad for that club, so a falling relegation chance is green and a rising one red; the arrow and sign always give the direction. With nothing to show, one dim line says so; without a baseline the block hides. A faint dotted rule on the chart marks the baseline Monday.
- **Match page** (`#match/<competition>/<fixture id>`): one page per fixture, under the Matches tab with no competition row, opened from "Match page →" in every expanded match and, on club pages, from a dim "→" at the end of each game row (its own tab stop; the rest of the row still expands) and from each call-strip bar. A "← Matches, <day>" crumb leads back. The hero names both clubs with 80 px crests (64 px on phones) and sets the favourite's chance as the poster numeral, labelled with the outcome ("Away win") and with the three outcome bulbs under it; a finished fixture shows the final score there instead. Below it, "The forecast" reuses the expanded match's likeliest scores, goals and call ("The forecast before kickoff" once played), "What's at stake" reuses the stakes table and hides when nothing passes the bar, and "The two sides" is one ruled board: home values right-aligned, the metric in the middle, away values left-aligned. Ratings carry rank plates and hairline meters with the average-club tick; last five are lettered result boxes with scores, oldest first; goals and xG per game state their xG coverage. Champions League clubs from outside the five leagues have no xG, so their side says so. An unknown fixture shows one note linking back to Matches.
- **Match page after full time:** a finished fixture with a forecast gets "After · Before" chips in its hero (After by default); Before is the pre-game page unchanged. After keeps the final score as the numeral and the outcome bulbs with the ruled box on the result, plus one sentence on the chance the model gave it and whether that forecast was published before kickoff or reconstructed. "Forecast against result" gives the actual score's chance and its place in the likeliest scores, the result's rank this season by the chance given to what happened (least or most expected, whichever is nearer), and a paired board with exact labels: "Expected before kickoff (model)", "Goals" and, where Understat has it, "Shot xG (Understat)". "What it changed" shows season chances at the refreshes either side of the game (both clubs' live races, plus rivals from stakes saved before kickoff) with ▲▼ changes, coloured green when good for that club and red when bad (a rising relegation chance is red), and a dim dash under 2 points; the stakes table as saved before kickoff, its "Before" column from the last refresh before the game and the result that happened lit; and "Strength": both clubs' rating, attack and defence from the last refresh before kickoff to the first whose results include the game, with a "League average" row for each club's league so a league-wide shift isn't read as the club's result (blank for clubs outside the five leagues). Its note names both refresh times in UTC; a game before rating runs were saved, or one no refresh has included yet, gets a one-line note instead. Each part hides without its data.
- **Shot report ("Chances"):** in a finished league game's After mode, between "Forecast against result" and "What it changed", when Understat has its shots; the Champions League has none. The **shot map** is one full pitch in `--rule` lines on `--panel`, home shooting right and away mirrored to shoot left, with the clubs named under it and arrows for direction. Circle area is the shot's xG; goals are filled amber bulbs, saved shots filled `--ink-2`, everything else outlined. Hover or tap shows the player, minute, xG, situation and outcome, and a `<details>` "Shot list" table under the map is its text equivalent. Own goals aren't chances: they stay off the map and out of the xG lines, and the note names them. The **xG timeline** draws cumulative shot xG as step lines from 0' to the last minute played, home solid ink and away dashed `--ink-2`, with the totals at the line ends and goals as amber bulbs on the scoring side's line, labelled with the minute and surname ("(og)" for an own goal, on the line of the side it counted for). Shots in the same minute combine as 1 − ∏(1 − xG) when that gives Understat's match xG, plain sums when those do; a side that matches neither is labelled "summed shot xG". "From the chances" gives the after-the-fact home/draw/away chances as three plain numbers, never bulbs, labelled as not a forecast; "Standouts" is a ruled board of the top three players by xG + xA.

Generated with `palette.mjs --seed 67.65 --chroma balanced --harmony mono --scheme dark`; `--dim` was lifted from the ramp's step 9 to clear 4.5:1 for small labels.

## Task 9 · Match notes

Each match page has one ruled “Notes” frame. Before mode labels it “On the line” and shows up to four fixed-template facts per side, led by a small lit amber bulb, followed by the six latest meeting score chips when available. Each chip exposes its UTC date and home venue on hover, focus, or tap. After mode labels the same frame “What it meant” and shows only notes about streaks extended or ended, a qualifying venue win, or a new season record. A dim line lists the exact source seasons, including any gaps. Empty note and meeting lists hide the frame. The notes use mono text, no new colour or motion, and load only on the match page.

## Task 12 · Match stats and timeline

Finished match pages add "Match stats" in After mode when ESPN detail is available. It opens like a broadcast scoreboard: both clubs with 36 px crests, the final score between them, and under each club its scorers (surname and minutes, "pen" or "og" tagged) and red cards, each led by its event icon. Possession follows as one full-width split bar with the two percentages at its ends. Below, up to nine rows place home values on the left and away values on the right around a centred label: shots, on target, corners, saves, pass accuracy, fouls, offsides and cards, plus Understat xG when the league has it. Each bar is that side's share of the pair, growing out from the label. The better figure in each row is lit: its value sits on an amber plate and its bar is amber, the other side dim; more is better except fouls, offsides and cards, where fewer is; a tie lights neither. The plate's shape and a spoken "(better)" mean colour never stands alone. Percentages keep their units and absent pairs hide their row.

Events have their own small icons, the one place outside relegation and calls where extra hues appear, each always beside text: a white football for goals (red for an own goal, grey and struck through in red for a missed penalty), yellow (`--yellow`) and red cards, and a green ▲ on / red ▼ off pair for substitutions. The "Timeline" feed lists every event in order, home events right-aligned to a centred minute pill and away events left-aligned, with dashed Kick-off, Half-time (with the score) and Full time markers. Goal rows light the minute pill amber and add the running score in a ruled box and the assister underneath; substitutions are quieter rows with the player on above the player off. Each row carries spoken text (event, club, on/off).

On the existing xG timeline, ESPN goals replace the fallback Understat markers and red cards add a red card glyph labelled "red card". Goals keep amber bulbs and minute/scorer labels. Without shots, including Champions League games, a compact ruled minute line puts home events above and away events below with the same icons and a dashed half-time rule; the feed is its text equivalent. Before mode and expanded match rows keep their existing presentation. The footer credits "ESPN · match statistics".

## Task 8 · Scorecard

Matches replaces the season record with “Scorecard · this season”: a ruled board with Competition, Played, amber Log loss, signed model-minus-frequency baseline, Brier, Called hit rate and Published share. A lower model score gets a green ▲ paired with its signed value. The board scrolls horizontally on phones. One note explains lower scores, the 1.099 uniform guess and the combined cohort, followed by “Calibration and the rolling record →”.

The Model view's “Track record” frame is reached by `#model/scorecard` within the existing Model tab. It sits after Club ratings and before Limits, so the Model page still opens on how the forecasts are made. A native competition select and Combined · Published · Reconstructed chips carry sample counts; a ruled summary retains exact-score rate and average chance given alongside log loss, Brier and RPS. Reliability uses amber dots sized by count, a dashed ink diagonal and a ruled grid. Cumulative log loss uses solid ink for the model, dashed ink for prior frequency and dotted secondary ink for Elo. Both charts have captions and expandable numeric tables. They sit side by side on desktop and stack at 375 px. Zero-published cohorts give their count and one note, with charts hidden. A coverage disclosure lists prior seasons and paired comparison counts; caveats distinguish receipts and reconstruction, calibration overlap and archived prices in Champions League strengths. No new motion, shared selector changes or section tab.

## Task 5 · Games that matter

Matches has a "Games that matter · next 7 days" frame between the day strip and the chosen day's fixtures. It covers every competition whatever the chips say, and lists up to three unplayed fixtures from the next seven days that have a Stakes tag, ranked by their biggest shown swing (highest minus lowest of the three conditional chances) as a multiple of that race's Stakes bar, so an 8-point title bar and a 30-point Champions League bar count alike; an earlier kickoff wins a tie. Rows are ordinary match rows that expand into the usual panel; the kickoff column shows the competition code, the day and the time. One dim mono line under each row gives the swing as the chance now and under the result that moves it furthest, e.g. "Title: Barcelona 93% → 84% if they lose." The bars are the existing Stakes bars. The frame is hidden when no fixture qualifies. No new colours or motion.

## Task 13 · UEFA running data and activity maps

Finished Champions League match pages add Distance (km), Sprints and Top speed (km/h) to the existing Match stats frame. They use the paired bars with units at the ends; neither side is marked better for running activity. The existing ten-row limit expands by these three rows only for UEFA pages.

One new frame, "Where they played", follows Match stats in After mode when UEFA heat data exists. It opens with one control row, every control at chip size (40 px, 44 px on touch) with the chip border and lettering: two club chips with crests (pressed = amber), a chip-styled native Player select (Whole team, or anyone with 30 minutes or more; amber when a player is chosen, with a visually hidden label) and a Positions toggle chip. On phones the club chips share the first row and the select and toggle the second. The map uses the shot map's pitch markings (`pitchLines`), in ink over a continuous green → yellow → red activity surface (`--heat-lo`, `--heat-mid`, `--heat-hi`, used only here): green is less activity and red is more, relative to the busiest cell of the selected map. The saved 21×14 counts are bilinearly interpolated in native Canvas before colour is applied. This heatmap is an explicit exception to the single-amber bulb signature, requested on 14 October 2026. Under the pitch, the shot map's direction line names the club and its attacking direction; `.mp-key` rows give the scale ("Less → More activity", plus the starter dot when shown) and the defensive, middle and attacking-third shares. Positions shows numbered starter dots (average locations, not the formation); with a player chosen, that player's dot gets an amber ring and the others dim. A "Starters' average positions" `<details>` board and the SVG's spoken label are the text equivalents, and each grid cell's native tooltip gives its unsmoothed share. Side changes reset to Whole team; focus stays on the control used. Before mode and fixtures without UEFA data hide the frame. The footer credits "UEFA · match data". No new motion.

Own-goal timeline labels name both the scorer's club and the beneficiary, e.g. "53' Own goal · Renato Veiga (Villarreal), for Dortmund". The feed states the same relationship, while the ruler marker and running score stay on the side the goal counted for.

## Task 2 · Club Squad view

Every club page has an **Overview · Squad** chip pair under its hero. They are links, not toggles: Overview is `#clubs/<slug>` (or the open `/vs/<other>` comparison) and Squad is `#clubs/<slug>/squad`, so direct links and back/forward move between the views; the open one carries `aria-current="page"` and the amber chip border. Squad hides the overview frames and loads `squads/<season>/<slug>.json` only when opened; without that file one frame says "Squad data isn't available for this club yet."

"Ideal XI · EA ratings" opens with a `.mp-key` row (formation, mean EA overall, rated players of the squad) and a ruled "Provisional" plate when unrated squad members could change the XI. The pitch reuses `pitchLines`, turned upright like a team sheet (own goal at the bottom, left-sided players on the left) so surnames stay legible at 375 px. Each player is an unlit bulb with an amber ring and their EA overall in `--amber-t`, surname in caps below (a first initial when two share one); an unfilled role is a dashed `--dim` ring labelled "<role> unfilled". On desktop an ordered list of role, name and EA overall sits beside the pitch, below it on phones; it is the pitch's text equivalent. The note states the selection rule (best EA-rated XI from listed positions, everyone available), why the XI is provisional or which roles are unfilled, the EA edition and snapshot date, and that EA overall is EA's player rating, not Floodlight's club strength, with a gap never meaning goals or a win chance.

"Squad · EA ratings" is one horizontal scroller of ruled `board` tables, one per line (Goalkeepers, Defenders, Midfielders, Attackers, then any without a listed position) under `dgroup` headings with counts, sharing fixed column widths so the lines align. Rows sort by EA overall: name (sticky, with an outlined "XI" badge for ideal-XI players), age on the roster date, EA positions, OVR in `--amber-t`, and the six face stats; goalkeepers' columns are EA's keeper stats (DIV HAN KIC REF SPD POS). Face stats of 80 or more are bold ink and below 60 dim, never a new hue. Unmatched or ambiguous players stay in the table as dim italic "not rated". The footer credits "EA SPORTS FC · player ratings". No new colours or motion.

## Task 5 · Squad playing time

When a squad file has `usage`, the Ideal XI frame opens with two chip groups: one chip per competition (league first, then Champions League), each carrying its covered/played game count, and an **Ideal · Most used** pair. Most used is the default whenever the chosen competition has a most-used XI and covered games; changing competition keeps the mode unless that XI is missing. Every view shows one competition at a time; league and Champions League minutes are never added together. The legend becomes "Most used XI · <competition>", and the `.mp-key` row adds "Covered n/m games".

Each dot's ring is a minutes meter: a `--rule` track that fills clockwise in amber with the player's share of available minutes (minutes ÷ 90 per covered game; UEFA minutes include stoppage time, so a Champions League share can pass 100% while the ring stops full). The EA overall stays inside the dot. The text list adds the share before EA overall.

The squad table adds ST, SUB, MIN and MIN% after EA pos, separated by a hairline, plus xG90 and xA90 (Understat, from 90 minutes up) for league cohorts only. A player missing from the cohort shows dashes: unknown, never zero. The sticky name column and OVR stay in view at 375 px; the rest scrolls.

Task 4b adds regular match-sheet players missing from the listed squad. A dim, wrapping note under their name in the table and most-used XI list says "seen in match sheets, not in the listed squad". Rated observations use the same EA numbers and position groups; unrated ones remain visible with "not rated". The table note explains the two-game threshold, name-based EA match and unknown current membership. They contribute to playing time and the rated most-used XI, while the ideal XI and rated/squad counts still describe only the listed squad. No new colours or controls.

"XI history · <competition>" is a ruled board, newest first: date, opponent (crest and name), a starters strip, ideal-XI starters n/11, rotation (starters changed since the previous game with all eleven starters identified, or "Unchanged", "Starters not all identified", "–") and an amber "→" link to the match page ("Heatmap →" for covered Champions League games). The strip is eleven 8 px bulbs ordered goalkeeper to attack: amber for ideal-XI players, unlit for others, dashed `--dim` for starters not in the current squad or not identified; a visually hidden sentence names every starter. Games without a sheet say "No match sheet for this game". Without `usage`, the view is unchanged. No new colours or motion.

## Evaluation plan Task 2 · Download cards

Four pages offer a "Download card" chip pair (Portrait 1080×1350, Landscape 1200×675) behind a dim "Download card" label; on phones the label takes its own line so the two chips sit together. Each chip has a full spoken label ("Download full-time card, portrait, 1080 by 1350 PNG"). `cards.js` draws the card on a canvas from data the page already has, after `document.fonts.ready`, and saves it as a PNG; nothing is posted anywhere. Crests are local files, so the canvas is never tainted. The page itself is each card's text equivalent.

Cards are a scoreboard frame on `--bg` with its legend cut into the top border, built only from the existing tokens (read from the stylesheet at draw time) and the two type families; no new hues. Hero blocks are centred, detail blocks are ruled boards. Portrait stacks everything in one column; landscape puts the hero on the left and the detail on the right of a `--rule-2` divider. Blocks shrink to fit, and text shrinks to 60% and then truncates, so the wide fallback fonts still fit. The footer carries the logo's bulb grid, the Floodlight wordmark, the site address in `--amber-t` and a dim line crediting the sources.

- **Match preview** (match page, Before mode, and every upcoming fixture): the stage legend, kickoff day and time in UTC, both crests and names, the favourite's chance as the poster numeral, the three outcome bulbs with percentages, expected goals, the most likely score and its chance, the ruled Stakes tag with the race it swings when the fixture has one, and the model run's time.
- **Full time** (match page, After mode): the final score as the numeral; the pre-match bulbs headed "published before kickoff" or "reconstructed", with the ruled box on the result; a Called/Missed lamp with its word and the chance given to what happened; the on-chances home/draw/away shares from two independent Poissons with the observed xG as means, as plain numbers labelled "from xG, not a forecast" (a dim line says when there's no xG, as in the Champions League); expected goals and Understat xG. Under the lamp, the match page's verdict line from the exported `miss` ("Upset; Newcastle United had the better chances"); without `miss` the slot stays empty. The on-chances shares use the exported `xg_p` when present.
- **How we did** (Matches, the chosen day under its title, when it has finished games with a forecast): the scope (one competition's stage or "All competitions", following the chips), calls right out of games as the numeral, one bulb per game (green called, `--draw` grey a draw, red an upset, with a worded key), misses split into draws (the favourite drew) and upsets, mean log loss for Floodlight, Elo and the 1.099 uniform guess with the lower of the first two lit amber, the best call and the biggest upset with crests and the chance given, and the published/reconstructed count.
- **The race** (Tables, under "The race so far"): the run date and simulation count, the title favourite's chance as the numeral (red for relegation), then title, top-four and relegation lists (winner, top 8 and top 24 in the Champions League) of up to four clubs: the likeliest for the title and the drop, the four nearest 50% for place races. Each row has crest, name, chance and the whole-point change since the movers' baseline Monday as a drawn ▲▼ with a signed number, green when good for that club and red when bad, and a dim dash under 3 points.

## Evaluation plan Task 4 · How we miss

The Track record frame adds **How we miss** (`#model/misses`) after the calibration and cumulative charts, before the baselines. It follows the competition select and the Combined · Published · Reconstructed chips, and hides when the scorecard has no `misses`. A lead line gives calls that came true and the misses by kind. Two pairs sit side by side on desktop and stack at 375 px:

- **Called against result:** a ruled `board`, rows the call and columns the result, each cell a count with its dim row share; the diagonal of calls that came true is lit amber. A call nobody made shows dashes.
- **The misses, by chances:** one split meter per kind (draws, upsets, and missed draw calls only when nonzero), all on one scale of total misses, each split by whether the favourite won, was level (within 0.5 xG) or lost on whole-game observed xG. Shades step down the existing tokens (amber, `--dim`, `--rule`) and no xG is a dashed outline; a worded key and a dim line under each bar ("won 23 · level 27 · lost 25; 4 without xG") are the text equivalent. The note states xG coverage; the Champions League says Understat has no xG for it.
- **Expected against actual:** home, draw and away rows with calls, expected count, actual count and the signed difference.
- **Goals:** one line with expected goals before kickoff, goals and xG per team-game over the games with xG, and both mean absolute errors.
